A day in the life of Pragma on your team

  1. 0

    Pragma knows your business.

    Before the first story, Pragma builds a map of your business capabilities and the code behind them, and confirms that understanding with you. Every story is read against that picture, not in isolation.

  2. 1

    A story is assigned.

    Someone adds the Pragma label to a story in your project management tool. Pragma picks it up the way an assigned engineer would.

  3. 2

    Pragma reviews it.

    Pragma reads the story and the acceptance criteria, checks them against its understanding of your business and your codebase, and drafts a technical approach. Gaps, risks and conflicts become questions, not assumptions.

  4. 3

    Pragma clarifies with your product owner.

    In the story's thread in your team chat, Pragma asks what is unclear. It works through the answers, a little back and forth, until the story is understood.

  5. 4

    Pragma confirms the approach with your developer.

    With the clarifications in hand, Pragma refines the technical approach. It talks it through with the assigned developer in the same messaging tool until the developer confirms it.

  6. 5

    Pragma does the work, and asks for help when stuck.

    Pragma implements in its own isolated workspace. When it hits a problem it cannot resolve, it reaches out to the developer in the channel, the way a teammate would, then carries on.

  7. 6

    Pragma opens the pull request and tells the team.

    Pragma verifies the change, opens the pull request, and notifies the developer and the team in the channel. Review comments, change requests and CI failures come back to it until the merge.
